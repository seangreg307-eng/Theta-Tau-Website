    • The Beginnings

    The Rutgers colony of Theta Tau was founded by a student group of engineers and were established on October 18, 2009. On that day, Mike Abraham, the Executive Director of Theta Tau from the Central Office in Texas visited Rutgers and the original 8 members passed by-laws, elected officers, and scheduled meetings. The group also established their name of Eta Theta Tau or ΗΘΤ.

    The group met with the Dean of Student Affairs the next day and started talking about starting an engineering greek life group at Rutgers. We made note that we intended to be an active part of the School of Engineering by helping with service events, reaching out to freshman and participating in engineering competitions. Dean Brown advised us to find an advisor and meet with the Engineering Governing Council Board to discuss how to become a registered student org. On December 7th the group found its first advisor, Professor Blase Ur.

    Rutgers Theta Tau was established as a colony in April of 2010. The Founding class was comprised of 11 men and women who looked forward to bringing the Theta Tau experience and philosophy to the 8th oldest university in the United States. In September 2010, Blase Ur moved abroad to pursue a reserah opportunity. In his absence, Theta Tau petiotioned and recruited Jean Patrick Antoine, Assistant Dean in the School of Engineering as their new advisor.

    The fraternity has since grown to over 40 people in membership and recently initiated the Θ Class during the Fall 2013 semester.

    Since its inception as Eta Theta Tau, this student group has made many strides towards becoming a leading organization in the Rutgers School of Engineering. From participating in community service events to hosting professional development events, Rutgers Theta Tau is continually seeking an opportunity to make a difference on campus, in the community, and grow stronger as individuals and united in fellowship. Some of the major community service events the group participates in are: The Big Chill – A Charity Race to Help Kids, Rutgers Dance Marathon, Relay for Life, Blood Drive and others in cause for Habitat for Humanity. During Fall 2011, Rutgers Theta Tau successfully hosted Entrepreneurship Day with Steve Woznaik, co-founder of Apple, as its keynote speaker. Theta Tau brings various companies such as Lockheed Martin, Goldman Sachs and Avanade on campus to interact with students. The Rube Goldberg Machine Contest is hosted annually while they also take part in Engineering Olympics.
